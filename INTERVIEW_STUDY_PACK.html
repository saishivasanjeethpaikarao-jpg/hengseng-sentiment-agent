Your 60-second pitch

“I built a Python research prototype for five Hong Kong and five Korean stocks. It collects public discussion, headlines and daily prices, normalizes the records, aligns timestamps to each exchange’s local cutoff, and creates next-session return labels. I tested a Ridge baseline with expanding chronological folds and a one-session purge, then compared it with predicting zero return. The Ridge model did not beat that no-change benchmark in either market. The sentiment experiment is tiny and also does not establish an edge. Because the run had a $0 limit, I used clearly labelled public-source and RSS substitutes rather than claiming to have used Apify or Exa. My next steps are provider compliance through a verified no-charge path, fuller Hong Kong coverage, and a manually checked multilingual sentiment benchmark.”

Two-minute structure

  1. Problem: explore whether investor discussion can add information for next-session returns across ten example stocks.
  2. Data: 1,499 social rows, 110 RSS headlines, 4,875 third-party price bars and ten timestamped quote snapshots.
  3. Method: canonical event records, exchange-local cutoff, next-session log-return target, fixed Ridge model.
  4. Validation: dates stay together, train precedes test, one target step is purged, preprocessing fits on train only.
  5. Finding: no-change is slightly better in the long price-only test; short sentiment results have only three test dates per market.
  6. Next: solve exact provider/source requirements at $0, improve HK coverage and historical as-of data, then re-run the frozen evaluation.
Tip: Be clear that the negative result is informative. Explain your method and the limitation before speculating about a future improvement.

Sentiment score in one line

(positive keyword hits − negative keyword hits) / total matched hits

No word matched can produce zero, which is not the same as verified neutral sentiment. The scorer can miss negation, irony, slang and context.

Timezone explanation

Events are stored in UTC, converted to Asia/Hong_Kong or Asia/Seoul for cutoff alignment. Naïve Naver/Daum timestamps are interpreted as Seoul local; source-clock accuracy and the example exchange calendars still require independent verification.

Interview questions and safe answers

Try to answer aloud before expanding each response.

Did your model predict accurately?
“The long price-only Ridge model was slightly worse than predicting zero return in both markets. I did not establish improvement or profitability. The latest quote forecasts have not yet been evaluated.”
Did sentiment help?
“The sentiment test had 15 rows across only three dates per market. It beat the paired Ridge on that tiny sample but lost to the zero-return baseline. It is exploratory, not evidence of a durable edge.”
Did you use Apify and Exa?
“No, not in this $0 run. I used local public-source collectors and RSS substitutes, and documented the provider-compliance gap. Code adapters alone do not mean those services were used.”
How did you avoid leakage?
“I split chronologically by date, keep all tickers on a date together, purge the boundary so a training target cannot cross into test time, fit preprocessing only on training data, and exclude retrospective cumulative engagement as a predictor.”
Why use Ridge?
“It is a transparent regularized benchmark for a small feature set. I fixed alpha rather than tune on the test outcomes. A complex model would not solve incomplete or leaking data.”
What is the largest data limitation?
“Hong Kong social coverage is only 24 Moomoo posts; no LIHKG records were retained and AIA has no social sample. Korean counts are uneven. The bounded latest-page sample is not an exhaustive archive.”
What would you do next?
“First close the named provider/source requirement using a verified no-charge route. Then collect longer as-of history, verify official closes and exchange calendars, annotate a balanced multilingual sentiment sample, and repeat the same frozen chronological test.”
What did you build yourself?
Answer truthfully about the parts you personally ran and can explain. You can say you used coding assistance; then demonstrate ownership by walking through a module and reproducing a test. Do not claim unaided authorship of every generated line.
Do not claim: profitability, proven sentiment lift, complete ten-stock coverage, use of Apify/Exa, official exchange closes, or correct future forecasts.
